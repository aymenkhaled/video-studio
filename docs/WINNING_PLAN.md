# WINNING PLAN: what to make, why people will watch, and how YouTube will push it
*(Deep research, 13 Nov 2026. Written as if it were my own money and time. Kids' content is excluded, as Aymen asked.)*

---

## 1. Why our videos failed (honest diagnosis)
1. **No packaging.** We never designed a thumbnail or tested a title. For long videos, the thumbnail and title decide whether anyone clicks. For Shorts, the first second decides whether people swipe away.
2. **Shorts on a brand-new channel.** YouTube shows a new Short to a small "seed audience" first and widens it only if those viewers keep watching. YouTube's Shorts lead calls early views "exploratory". Ours lost the seed test: swipes in the first 1–2 seconds and an unclear subject.
3. **A synthetic voice plus a templated look.** This is exactly what YouTube's 2026 "inauthentic content" enforcement targets: synthetic narration, templated visuals and minimal editorial input.
4. **A niche too narrow and slow.** Dinosaur *sounds* has maybe 12–15 episodes in it, and we made 1 video every few days at 2–4 hours of rendering each.
5. **No audience research.** We picked topics we liked instead of topics that already prove demand: videos far outperforming their channel's size.
6. **No identity.** Nothing told a viewer why to subscribe ("what do I get next week?").

## 2. How YouTube decides (the rules of the game)
- **Satisfaction, not just clicks.** YouTube surveys a sample of viewers (1–5 stars) and predicts the rest. Only 4–5 star watch time counts as "valued". Likes, shares, "Not interested" and "Don't recommend channel" all feed in. Clickbait that doesn't pay off gets punished.
- **Watch time:** both absolute minutes and the percentage watched, judged differently for different lengths (Todd Beaupré, YouTube, Sept 2026).
- **Shorts:**
  - Seed audience first, then wider or stop. "The audience is the algorithm."
  - The key metric is **viewed vs swiped away**.
  - **Don't repost** a failed Short: it can be flagged as spam.
  - Since 1 Oct 2026, re-uploaded content from others gets reduced reach.
  - The Shorts dislike button was removed on 25 Jun 2026, so "Not interested" is now the negative signal.
- **A/B testing (Test & Compare):** up to 3 titles and thumbnails on long videos, on desktop, with advanced features enabled. The winner is chosen on **watch time**, not CTR.
- **Auto-dubbing and multi-language audio:** open to all creators in good standing. Your English video can reach Spanish, Portuguese, French, Hindi and other audiences. Review each dub before publishing.

## 3. The policy minefield (2026–2027)
- **Inauthentic / mass-produced content.** Enforcement waves in Jan 2026, with the policy clarified around Jul 2026. Flagged patterns: *templated slideshows, synthetic voices, repeated formats, minimal editorial intervention, stock-footage loops*. Even some honest faceless creators got caught. **Our protection:**
  - A human voice: yours.
  - Original research and argument in every script.
  - Custom visuals for each video.
  - No identical template where only the topic changes.
- **AI disclosure:** use the "altered or synthetic" toggle for realistic AI content. Since 27 May 2026, YouTube auto-labels undisclosed photorealistic AI. Our stylised maps and art don't need it, but never fake realistic footage.
- **Monetization thresholds:**
  - **Until 31 Jan 2027:** 1,000 subscribers plus 4,000 watch hours (12 months) or 10M Shorts views (90 days).
  - **From 1 Feb 2027:** 8,000 hours or 20M Shorts views.
  - **Shorts revenue for all partners from 2027:** only with at least 10M Shorts views per 90 days.
  - **Conclusion:** **long-form watch hours are the realistic route.** Shorts are for discovery only.
- **Copyright:** no film or TV clips, no news footage, no celebrity photos, no copyrighted music. Use only licensed or public-domain material and credit it.

## 5. The recommendation (one main channel, done well)
**Channel concept: "Maps that explain the world".** Animated-map explainers covering **history, geography and economics** in one brand. Every video answers one strong question with maps, timelines and data charts.
- Why it wins:
  - It uses the highest-paying niche (economics) and the most evergreen one (history).
  - The visuals are **exactly** what code does best: maps, borders morphing, data charts.
  - The format has proven demand: RealLifeLore (~7.9M), Economics Explained (~2.9M), Epic History (~3.1M), all mostly map and narration formats.
- **Your edge:** a Tunisian / North African and Mediterranean perspective. Carthage, the Berbers, the Ottoman Maghreb, Saharan trade, the Arab world's geography and economy are big questions with far fewer quality English explainers than Rome or WWII. Mix 50% "big global questions" (proven demand) with 50% "your region" (less competition).
- **Voice: you.** Record the narration yourself in English. An accent is fine; many big explainer channels have one. It's the single strongest protection against "inauthentic content", and it builds trust. Use Kokoro only for drafts and timing. Auto-dub then gives you other languages for free.
- **Shorts:** 2–3 per long video, cut from it (one map moment plus one surprising fact, 20–40 s). They send viewers to the long video through a related-video link.
- **Second channel (later, optional, after 30 long videos):** an Arabic version with your voice, or the creatures rankings channel.

## 6. Why viewers will keep watching (retention design)
**Long video, 8–14 min:**
1. **0–15 s, hook.** Show the strongest map or image plus the question stated in one sentence, plus a promise ("by the end you'll see why…"). No intro, logo or "hey guys".
2. **15–60 s, stakes.** Why it matters to the viewer, and one surprising fact.
3. **Open loops.** Tease a payoff early ("the real reason is the third one") and pay it off late.
4. **Chapters every 1.5–2.5 min,** each one a mini-question and answer, so the pacing resets.
5. **Pattern interrupts every 20–40 s:** a map zoom, a chart, a quote card, a sound sting, a change of scene. Never more than 8 seconds of a static frame.
6. **Payoff and synthesis**, then a 5–10 s end screen pointing to the next video. **Don't** end with a long outro.
7. **Accuracy:** list sources in the description. Commenters correcting you destroys trust and satisfaction scores.

**Short, 20–40 s:**
- Frame 1 already has motion and the subject.
- An on-screen question in under 1 s.
- Payoff before 80% of the length.
- An end that loops into the start.
- Captions are mandatory (most people watch muted).

## 7. Packaging (the part we never did)
- **Titles:** under 60 characters, a curiosity gap plus a concrete subject ("Why Almost Nobody Lives in the Sahara"). No ALL CAPS, no lies.
- **Thumbnails:**
  - 1 subject plus at most 3 words.
  - High contrast, readable at phone size.
  - A map with one highlighted region and an arrow or symbol works well for this niche.
  - The same visual language every time, so the brand is recognisable.
  - Built in code: Remotion `still`, 1280×720, rendered alongside the video.
- **A/B test:** 3 thumbnail variants on every long video with Test & Compare. Write down the winner and learn the pattern.
- **Description:** 2 lines of hook, then chapters (timestamps), sources and credits.
- **Captions:** upload the corrected SRT produced by Whisper. It helps accessibility, search and dubbing.
- **Auto-dub:** turn it on, review the dubs and publish them.

## 8. Audience research before every video (new, required)
- **Find outliers:** videos with views at least 3× their channel's subscriber count, on our topic, in the last 2 years. That's proven demand.
- **Tools:**
  - **YouTube Data API v3:** free key, 10,000 units a day. A search costs 100 units, about 100 searches a day. OpenCode can script this: search a topic, get video stats and channel subscribers, and rank by views divided by subscribers.
  - YouTube search autocomplete (type the topic and read the suggestions).
  - The competitors' "Popular" tab.
  - Google Trends.
  - Optional free browser extensions: vidIQ and TubeBuddy free tiers (manual).
- **Rule:** no script until the topic has at least 2 outliers or a strong search suggestion.

## 10. Channel setup checklist (one time)
- Name and handle: short and clear, about maps and the world (OpenCode can suggest 10 names; check they're free).
- Banner, profile icon and description with keywords ("animated map explainers on history, geography and economics").
- Playlists by theme: North Africa, Empires, Economics, Geography.
- Default upload description template with a sources section.
- Enable advanced features (verify your phone) so you can use A/B testing.
- Audience setting: "No, not made for kids".
- Cross-post Shorts to TikTok and Instagram Reels.

## 11. Publishing rhythm and the analytics loop
- **Cadence:** 1 long video a week plus 2–3 Shorts cut from it. Quality over quantity: one excellent video a week beats seven templated ones, especially under the 2026 policy.
- **After 48 hours, check:**
  - CTR: aim for 4–8% or more on a new channel.
  - Average percentage viewed: aim for 35% or more on 10-minute videos.
  - The first-30-second retention drop: aim for 70% or more still watching at 30 s.
  - Shorts viewed-vs-swiped: aim for 70% or more.
- **Weekly:** pick the best and worst video, find where viewers left on the retention graph, and write one lesson into `LESSONS.md` so OpenCode reads it before the next script.

## 12. 90-day plan with honest gates
- **Weeks 1–2:**
  - Set up the channel.
  - Build the map engine, chart engine, thumbnail generator and voice pipeline.
  - Make video 1 and its 3 Shorts.
- **Weeks 3–6:** videos 2–5. A/B test every thumbnail.
- **Day 45 gate:**
  - **Continue** if any video has passed 2,000 views, or average view time is at least 35% with CTR at least 4%.
  - Otherwise change the **topic mix**, not the whole channel: lean toward whichever proved best, global or regional.
- **Day 90 gate:**
  - Over 400 watch hours: speed up to 2 videos a week.
  - Under 50 hours and no video above 1,000 views: rethink the niche, but keep the engines (they work for any map, data or history channel).
- **Honest odds:** reaching monetization before 31 Jan 2027 is unlikely (under 10%). Within 12 months, with consistent weekly quality videos in your own voice, my estimate is roughly 20–30%. Most channels quit before video 30; consistency is the real edge.
