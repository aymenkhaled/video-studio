# MASTER STRATEGY v2: every category, every scenario, critical view
*(13 Nov 2026. Goal: **maximum views**, built so no single decision can kill the business. Kids' content excluded.)*

---

## 1. Hard truths before choosing anything
1. **More channels doesn't mean safer.** YouTube's termination policy: *"If your YouTube channel is terminated, you are prohibited from using, possessing, or creating any other YouTube channels,"* and it applies to **all** your existing channels. One serious violation can take down every channel you own. Real safety comes from **compliance plus spreading across platforms** (YouTube, TikTok, Instagram, Facebook). Separate channels are worth having only because each niche needs its own audience signal.
2. **Views and money are different games.** Shorts bring views but little money: from 2027, Shorts pay only at 10M or more views per 90 days. Long videos bring money. Views-first is fine, but every Short must feed a long video.
3. **The 2026 enforcement targets exactly the easy path:** synthetic narration, templated thumbnails, stock-footage loops, mass-produced repeats. "Boring history for sleep" AI channels (2–3 h videos, millions of views) are the most visible example, criticised for fake facts. Copying that model is a short-lived gamble.
4. **Copyright is the fastest killer of faceless channels.** Studios issue *strikes*, not just claims, for movie and TV compilations. Three strikes in 90 days terminates the channel **and its associated channels**.
5. **Violence affects ad pay.** The 4 Sep 2026 update:
   - Graphic violence in the **first 7 seconds** means no ads.
   - Graphic means "brutal killings or severe injuries focusing on bodily fluids or body parts".
   - Mild, unrealistic or obscured violence is fine.
   - Videos made mainly to shock can be demonetized.
   - So **fights must be non-graphic and framed around science and strategy.**

## 3. The portfolio (3 brands, launched in order, each with its own style)
Each brand is a separate channel because each audience is different. They share one engine and repository.

### Brand A, launch first: "SCALE & POWER" (comparisons, rankings, versus)
- **Covers categories** 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 7, 9 and 14: everything about *the biggest, the deadliest, the rarest, who wins*.
- **Why first:**
  - It's the highest views per video for a faceless channel.
  - It doesn't depend on your voice: on-screen text, music and short narration work, and the format is universal across languages.
  - It's visual, and suits both Shorts and long videos.
- **Visual identity:** a 3D or 2.5D "measurement stage" (ground grid, human and bus for scale, height markers), clean labels and counters, smooth camera dollies along a lineup.
- **Long videos:** "Every Predator That Ever Lived, Ranked by Bite Force (60 min)", "Biggest Explosions in History, Size Compared". **Shorts:** single comparisons and versus moments.

### Brand B, launch 4–6 weeks later: "MAPS OF WAR & EMPIRE" (long videos, watch hours)
- **Covers categories** 6, 8, 12, 15 and 17: battles, wars, empires, lost civilizations, rise and fall.
- **Why:** long watch time and good RPM; animated maps are what our code does best.
- **Visual identity:** a parchment or satellite-style map, unit blocks moving in formations, arrows that draw themselves, smoke and dust, museum art in 2.5D parallax.
- **Voice:** your own, or Kokoro for drafts only. It's needed for long-form trust and policy safety.

### Brand C, later and only if A or B works: "DARK TALES" (myths, legends, original non-graphic horror)
- **Covers categories** 5, 13 and 12: high engagement, but ad suitability needs care.

### Spreading across platforms (real risk control)
- Every Short goes to TikTok, Instagram Reels and Facebook Reels as well.
- Long videos can go to Facebook too. Facebook's content monetization program pays for longer videos; check eligibility in your country.
- Keep an email list or page for the brand later. Something the platform can't take away.

## 4. Why a viewer watches, and keeps watching

| Format | The viewer's question | Retention mechanics |
|---|---|---|
| Size comparison | "How big is it, *really*, next to me?" | A constant scale reference (human, bus, Eiffel Tower), each item bigger than the last, a counter, a reveal of the giant at the end, never stopping the camera |
| Ranking (deadliest) | "What's number one?" | Countdown from 10 to 1, open loop ("number 1 isn't what you think"), each entry with **one shocking fact** plus a visual, 45–90 s per entry |
| Versus | "Who wins?" | Tale-of-the-tape stats card, three rounds (size, weapons, tactics), a hedged verdict, "comment who you think wins" (comments carry real weight) |
| War / battle map | "How did they win against those odds?" | Stakes first, then forces on the map, the plan, the twist, the turning point, then the consequence. Chapters every 2 min |
| Empire timeline | "How did it get that big and then die?" | Borders growing year by year, rulers as portrait cards, "the mistake that doomed them" as the open loop |
| Myths / horror | "What is it? Is it real?" | Cold open on the creature's silhouette, the story, the real-world origin of the myth, then the twist |

**Packaging rules:**
- One clear subject in the thumbnail, at most 3 words.
- Scale formats show *the two things side by side* in the thumbnail.
- Versus thumbnails show both fighters facing each other with "VS".
- Titles are concrete: "Every Predator Ranked by Bite Force", "Megalodon vs Blue Whale".

## 5. Making it look good: realistic, smooth, attractive motion

### What's realistic for free, on CPU
| Technique | Look | How | Speed (measured or estimated) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **3D creatures without post-processing** | Real anatomy, real animation clips, smooth camera | Remotion + three.js. Quaternius CC0 animated animals and dinosaurs, plus Poly Haven HDRI lighting, ambientCG PBR ground, fog | **Measured** ~1.4 s per frame on 2 vCPU (episode 2) |
| **2.5D parallax from photos and paintings** | Paintings "come alive" with camera depth | Depth-Anything-V2-Small depth map, then a three.js plane with displacement or 3–5 layers cut with rembg, then a slow dolly and orbit | Estimated 0.2–0.5 s per frame (1 plane, no post) |
| **Animated maps** | Kings and Generals style | d3-geo plus a virtual camera (pan and zoom along eased Bezier paths), units as sprites in formations, arrows drawn with `@remotion/paths`, particle smoke | **Measured** ~10 fps for 2D on 2 vCPU |
| **Cut-out character animation** | Soldiers and people from engravings moving | Public-domain engraving, rembg cut-out, parts split in Inkscape (one-time), then rotations around pivots | 2D speed |
| **Transitions** | Pro editing feel | `@remotion/transitions` (slide, wipe, fade, flip, clock wipe), match cuts, whip pans with directional streaks | 2D speed |
| **Motion blur** | Smooth fast action | `@remotion/motion-blur` (`<CameraMotionBlur>`). **It multiplies render cost by the sample count**, so use it only on fast moves of 1–2 s | Costly; use sparingly |
| **Atmosphere** | Cinematic depth | Fog, dust, embers, rain, god rays, light flicker, vignette and grain from **pre-made PNGs** (no per-frame blur) | Cheap |

### What is **not** possible for free
- Photorealistic film-quality humans and battles, and AI video generation (Veo, Kling, Runway and similar). These are paid or need a GPU.
- **If money becomes available later:** use a little paid AI B-roll only for establishing shots, mark it as AI, and never show realistic real people.

### Motion rules (in every prompt to the agent)
- Nothing is ever static for more than 3 s: the camera always drifts, even 1–2% a second.
- Every move is eased (in-out cubic or bezier); **no linear moves**.
- Cut or change the visual every 4–8 s in Shorts and every 6–15 s in long videos.
- Keep 1–3 depth layers moving at different speeds at all times (parallax).
- Animate sound and picture together (whoosh on transitions, impact on reveals).

## 6. Hours-long videos: time estimation and how to render them

### Formula
`render_minutes ≈ frames ÷ (fps_per_job × 60) ÷ parallel_jobs + overhead (about 5 min per job)`
- Frames = seconds × 24 (use **24 fps** for documentaries: 20% fewer frames than 30 fps).
- fps_per_job: **measure it** with a benchmark job on GitHub's runner. Our measurements on 2 vCPU: 2D ≈ 10 fps, 3D without post ≈ 0.7 fps.

### Estimates (2D-dominant episode, about 10 fps per job; verify with a benchmark)
| Length | Frames (24 fps) | 1 job | 20 parallel jobs |
|---|---|---|---|
| 10 min | 14,400 | ~24 min | ~6 min (setup dominates) |
| 1 hour | 86,400 | ~2.4 h | ~12 min |
| 3 hours | 259,200 | ~7.2 h (**over the 6 h job limit**) | ~27 min |

3D segments are about 14× slower, so keep 3D to short clips inside 2D episodes, or render a **3D clip library once** and reuse it.

### How (GitHub Actions)
- **Public repository** gives free, unlimited minutes on 4 CPU / 16 GB runners. **Private** gives 2,000 minutes a month on 2 CPU / 8 GB, which is about one 3-hour video a month at most.
  - **Decision:** for hours-long content, use a public repo with no secrets in the code (store keys as GitHub Secrets). The code and CC0 assets being visible is acceptable.
- **Limits:**
  - Up to **20 concurrent jobs** on the free plan.
  - A matrix can create up to 256 jobs.
  - Each job can run at most **6 hours**.
- **Remotion distributed rendering:**
  - Each matrix job runs `renderMedia` with its own `frameRange` (equal chunk sizes), codec `h264-ts`, and `forSeamlessAacConcatenation: true`.
  - A final job downloads all chunks and calls `combineChunks()` with codec `h264`.
- **Cheaper still:** render each scene once and cache it (keyed by a hash of the scene's code and data). Re-render only changed scenes, and assemble with ffmpeg `concat` (seconds).

### Should we even make hours-long videos?
- ✅ **Compilations of our own episodes** ("Every Predator Ranked: 2-hour full collection", "Rome: the complete story"). Legitimate, high watch hours, and cheap once episodes exist.
- ⚠️ New 2–3 h originals only after a 15–20 min version proves strong retention (40% or more). A 3-hour video that people leave after 4 minutes hurts the channel.
- ❌ An AI "history for sleep" flood. It's oversaturated, criticised for fake facts, and a target of enforcement.

## 7. Where to build: here, Claude Code desktop, or OpenCode?

| Option | Pros | Cons | Use it for |
|---|---|---|---|
| **This cloud chat** | Strong model, research, can render short tests | Files vanish when the session ends; background tasks capped at 2 h; 2 CPUs; uses your Claude plan | Research, strategy, prototypes |
| **Claude Code desktop app (local session)** | Works directly in your folder and Git repo; files persist on your disk; same strong model | Uses the same Claude plan quota (no extra allowance); rendering locally would hit your weak laptop, so **renders must still go to GitHub Actions** | **Best for building the engine**, if you have Claude quota |
| **OpenCode + your GPT API** | Your own API budget; works in the same repo | Weaker results so far (the Maiasaura test); needs very explicit prompts | Routine episode production once the engine and templates are solid |

**Recommendation:** make the **GitHub repo the single source of truth**. Build the engines (comparison stage, map engine, parallax, transitions, distributed render) with the strongest model you can access, ideally Claude Code desktop in a local session. Then let OpenCode produce routine episodes from those templates and checklists. Any tool can continue at any time because everything lives in the repo.

## 9. Scenarios and risk register
| Scenario | Probability | Early sign | Response |
|---|---|---|---|
| Nothing takes off in 30 days | High | <500 views per video | Keep producing; test 3 formats (ranking, versus, size), and double down on the best by views per impression |
| One video goes viral, then the channel dies | Medium | One video gets 90% of views | Make 5 follow-ups in the same format within 2 weeks; build a series and playlist; turn it into a long compilation |
| Demonetized for inauthentic content | Medium (synthetic voice, templates) | YPP rejection reason | Your voice on long videos, visible research and sources, varied visuals; never upload near-duplicates |
| Copyright claim or strike | Low with our rules | A claim email | Only CC0 / PD / own music; keep `LICENSES.md` to dispute; never movie or TV material |
| Ad limits on violent topics | Medium for versus and war | Yellow icon | No gore; nothing graphic in the first 7 s; educational framing; blur any injury |
| Termination (all channels) | Low if rules are followed | Strikes | Compliance first; cross-post to TikTok, Instagram and Facebook so the audience isn't only on YouTube |
| Competitors copy the format | High | Similar channels appear | Quality and accuracy, a recognizable style, speed of series production |
| Burnout or no time | Medium | Skipped weeks | Templates, distributed rendering, 1 long video and 4–6 Shorts a week maximum |
| Algorithm change | Always | View drop on all videos | Track viewed-vs-swiped and retention weekly; adapt hooks; spread across platforms |
