# Scale & Science 002 — topic research (15 Nov 2026)

Goal (Aymen): a rare, special topic with big-audience potential; not the usual ideas everyone knows. Must also fix the 001 weakness: real images and footage, not silhouettes.

## Recommended: "The 481-Metre Wave" (Tracy Arm, Alaska, 10 Aug 2025)
- What: a glacier-weakened mountainside (≥ 63.5 million m³) fell into Tracy Arm fjord; the water ran 481 m up the opposite slope, taller than the Empire State Building. Second-highest wave run-up ever recorded, after Lituya Bay 1958 (524 m). Nobody died: the fjord, normally visited by cruise ships, was empty at 05:26. Kayakers camped nearby had their gear swept away.
- Why rare: peer-reviewed only in May 2026 (Shugar et al., Science, doi 10.1126/science.aec3187); news coverage, but no big explainer video found.
- Hooks: a wave taller than a skyscraper · it happened last year · in a fjord full of tourists · nobody saw it · climate change (glacier retreat) makes more of them.
- Realism: USGS helicopter photos + video (data release doi 10.5066/P14SPXJG; USGS field photos marked Public Domain), NASA Earth Observatory before/after satellite images (PD), USGS 1958 Lituya Bay photos (PD), NOAA/USGS maps. Scale stage: wave height vs person, bus, Statue of Liberty, Eiffel Tower, Empire State Building, Burj Khalifa.
- Story arc: the empty morning → how a landslide makes a megatsunami → Lituya Bay 1958 (the survivors on a boat) → ranking of the tallest waves ever (run-up vs ocean tsunami vs rogue wave vs biggest surfed wave) → why retreating glaciers set up more → cruise-ship warning → question to viewers.
- Sources: USGS program page https://www.usgs.gov/programs/landslide-hazards/science/2025-tracy-arm-landslide-generated-tsunami · UAF/GI release https://www.gi.alaska.edu/news/study-2025-alaska-landslide-and-tsunami-contains-warnings · NASA https://science.nasa.gov/earth/earth-observatory/tracy-arms-post-tsunami-landscape/ · KTOO https://www.ktoo.org/2026/05/08/we-should-be-acting-more-quickly-tracy-arm-tsunami-highlights-unseen-hazards-in-busy-alaska-fjords/ · Lituya Bay: USGS Professional Paper 354-C (Miller 1960).
- Check before use: exact licence of the USGS aerial video release; non-graphic rule (no deaths here; Lituya Bay deaths mentioned as data only).

## Alternative 2: "The Biggest Living Thing Isn't a Whale" (sequel to 001)
- One seagrass plant 180 km long (Posidonia australis, Shark Bay; Edgeloe et al. 2022, Proc. R. Soc. B, ~4,500 years old); Pando aspen clone (~6,000 t, 47,000 trunks); Oregon honey fungus (~9.6 km²); a 45+ m siphonophore seen at Ningaloo (2020).
- Strong sequel hook to episode 001 ("the blue whale is the biggest ANIMAL… but not the biggest living thing"). Images: USFS (PD) Pando, NOAA Ocean Exploration (PD) siphonophores; Schmidt Ocean imagery needs a licence check (often NC).

## Alternative 3: "One Ant Colony Across Three Continents"
- Argentine ant supercolonies: European colony ~6,000 km along the Mediterranean; workers from Europe, Japan and California don't fight (Sunamura et al. 2009). Very surprising, but the science is from 2009 and "one global colony" needs careful hedging. Images: USDA/USGS PD ant macro photos.
